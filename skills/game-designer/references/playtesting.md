# Playtesting

Use for designing a test or interpreting actual observations. A plan, simulation, expert opinion, and completed playtest are different kinds of evidence.

## Turn uncertainty into a test

1. State the hypothesis in player terms: what participants should understand, attempt, feel, or choose.
2. Identify the smallest prototype or activity that can expose a failure of that hypothesis. Name what this test cannot establish.
3. Choose participants relevant to the target audience. State recruitment limitations and whether they already know the game.
4. Describe the starting setup, task, duration, facilitator behavior, and observations to collect. Avoid teaching the solution when testing discoverability; record help that was given.
5. Set a decision rule before testing where practical: evidence that would support, weaken, or leave the hypothesis unresolved. Treat early thresholds as provisional, not universal benchmarks.

For example: to test whether a charged attack creates a positioning decision, observe when players choose it over a fast attack, what makes them cancel, and whether they understand exposure during preparation. Damage totals alone cannot establish that the decision is interesting.

## Collect evidence that fits the question

- Understanding: mistaken predictions, missed cues, requests for help, or a player's explanation in their own words.
- Decisions: option use in context, avoidable failures, repeated strategies, and whether players notice tradeoffs.
- Pacing and progression: time to meaningful choice, stalled progress, retry behavior, and reasons to continue or stop.
- Experience: specific moments participants describe, supported by observed actions where possible. Do not reduce enjoyment to a single retention number.

Use neutral follow-up questions such as "What did you expect there?" Separate observed behavior, player interpretation, and the designer's explanation. Participation and recording should respect consent; do not require personal information irrelevant to the test.

## Interpret and iterate

Report the build, test conditions, participants, observations, and limitations. Counts from a small convenience sample describe that sample; they are not population estimates. Avoid claiming causation from a correlation or declaring victory from one enthusiastic player.

Separate implementation defects from unclear rules and experience problems. Prioritize the issue that invalidates the main hypothesis; propose the smallest useful change and what the next test should reveal. Change multiple variables together only when their interaction is the question being tested.

If no test occurred, deliver a plan and expected signals, not invented outcomes or claims that the design is validated.
